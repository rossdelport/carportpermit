Council approval

Carport council approval in WA

Short answer

Almost every carport in WA needs a building permit. It also needs planning approval when it doesn't meet the standard planning rules, most often because it is too close to the street, too wide or on a side boundary. Planning approval comes first.

Last updated 29 October 2026

Two kinds of approval

"Council approval" for a carport can mean two different things. A building permit checks the structure is safe and meets the building code. Planning approval (also called development approval) checks the carport suits the site and the street. Almost every carport needs a building permit. Some also need planning approval, and that one comes first.

When a carport needs planning approval

A carport only needs planning approval if it doesn't meet the standard ("deemed-to-comply") rules in the R-Codes and your council's local planning policies. The common reasons are:

  • Too close to the street. In front of the house, closer than the rules allow.
  • Too wide. More than 60% of the lot's frontage.
  • On a side boundary beyond the R-Codes' limits for boundary walls and structures.
  • Heritage areas and special estates with their own design rules.
  • Corner lots, where sightlines for cars and people matter.

Questions

Common questions

Does my neighbour have to agree to my carport?

Not usually. If planning approval is needed for something that affects them, such as a carport on the shared boundary, the council may ask them for comments. The council decides, not the neighbour.

Can I start building once planning approval is through?

No. You still need the building permit before work starts. Planning approval is the first step, not the last.
