City of Stirling carport rules

Short answer

In Stirling, a carport skips planning approval if it meets the street setback for your R-Code (3 m on R20, 2 m on R30), keeps posts 1 m from the side boundary, is no wider than 60% of the frontage and keeps the view to the street open. It always needs a building permit.

Last updated 3 November 2026

When you don't need planning approval

At the City of Stirling, a carport doesn't need development (planning) approval if it meets all of these:

  • it's at least the minimum distance from the street boundary (table below)
  • the posts are at least 1 m from the side boundary
  • enough open space is left on the lot (table below)
  • it's big enough to park in (minimum sizes below)
  • there's an unobstructed view between the house and the street
  • it's no wider than 60% of the lot frontage

Every carport needs a building permit, even when planning approval isn't needed.

Doors, corners and laneways

  • Doors. Any door must be visually permeable, such as a see-through tilt-up door.
  • Corner lots. Face the secondary street where you can, and avoid access from busy roads like Walcott Street or Karrinyup Road. You can build as close as 1.5 m to the secondary street.
  • Laneways. Access from a laneway is encouraged (Local Planning Policy 6.5).

Heritage areas and strata

All carports in the Heritage Protection Area (Mount Lawley, Menora and Inglewood) or on heritage-listed properties need planning approval, and must use the laneway if there is one. Strata lots may also need approval under the Strata Titles Act.

If you do need planning approval

The City has 60 days to decide, though carports are usually quicker. You'll need the application form signed by every owner, a title search from the last six months, the fee, a site plan at 1:200, floor plan and elevations at 1:100, and a written justification for anything that doesn't meet the rules. Sloping sites need a feature survey. You don't need your neighbour's approval to apply.

Questions

Common questions

Do I need council approval for a carport in Stirling?

You always need a building permit. You only need planning approval as well if the carport doesn't meet the setback, side boundary, open space, size, view and 60% width rules, or if it's in the Heritage Protection Area.

How close to the street can a carport be in Stirling?

It depends on your R-Code: 3 m on R20 and R25, 2 m on R30 and R40, 1 m on R50 and R60, and 0.5 m on R80.

Can I put a door on a carport in Stirling?

Only a visually permeable one, such as a see-through tilt-up door.

How long does a carport planning application take in Stirling?

The City has up to 60 days, but carports are normally faster.
