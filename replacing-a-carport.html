Question answer

Do I need approval to replace an old carport?

Short answer

Usually yes. The new carport is new building work, so it needs a building permit and must meet today's setback rules. Taking down a small freestanding carport usually needs no permit, unless it's over 40 m², brick, heritage-listed or has asbestos.

Last updated 21 November 2026

Taking the old one down

Old carportDemolition permit?
Freestanding, under 40 m², no asbestos, not brick, not heritageUsually no
Attached to the house, brick, heritage-listed, or has asbestosUsually yes

That's how the Town of Cottesloe sets it out; councils vary. Old carport roofs can contain asbestos. Take care and follow Department of Health and WorkSafe guidance before you touch it.

Putting the new one up

A new carport is new building work, even in the same spot. It usually needs a building permit unless it's freestanding, no more than 10 m² and 2.4 m high.

It's assessed under today's rules, not the rules when the old one was built. If the old carport was never approved, the new one doesn't get any credit for it. The R-Codes rules that most often matter:

  • Front setback: a carport can usually sit at half the normal street setback if it's no wider than 60% of the frontage, keeps a clear view of the house, and matches the house's roof, colours and materials.
  • Boundary: posts can usually go on the side boundary if the carport is no more than 10 m long and 2.7 m high, behind the street setback, with the roof and gutters at least 450 mm off the boundary.

If it breaks one of these, you'll usually need planning approval first.

Just replacing the roof sheets?

Building and Energy says changing a roof cover to a material of a different weight or shape will likely need a building permit, and the roof must be tied down properly. Some councils, such as Cottesloe, list even like-for-like re-roofing as needing a permit. Ask your council first.

Since 1 July 2026, a carport job under $50,000 doesn't need a registered builder.

Questions

Common questions

Do I need a permit to replace my carport in WA?

Usually yes. A new carport is new building work and needs a building permit unless it's freestanding, 10 m² or less and 2.4 m or less high.

Do I need a permit to demolish an old carport?

Usually not if it's freestanding, under 40 m², not brick, not heritage-listed and has no asbestos. Otherwise you usually do.

Can I rebuild my carport in the same spot?

Only if the new one meets today's setback and boundary rules, or you get planning approval.

Does re-roofing a carport need approval?

It can. Changing to a different roof material will likely need a building permit, and some councils need one even for like-for-like sheets.
