Attached vs freestanding carport

Short answer

Both need a building permit. An attached carport's plans must show how it connects to the house, with engineering. A freestanding one needs its own footings and must meet the boundary and fire-separation rules. The planning rules (setbacks, 60% frontage, open view) are the same for both.

Both always need a building permit

The Cities of Stirling and Wanneroo both say every carport needs a building permit. The state's 10 m² exemption is for freestanding patios, sheds and gazebos, and a carport big enough to park in is larger than that anyway.

Attached carports

An attached carport ties into the house, so the plans need connection details to the roof frame and footings, and structural engineering. At Wanneroo, for example, attached roofed structures like patios need these details on every application. The R-Codes definition of a carport allows it to be closed in only where it meets the house or a boundary on one side.

Freestanding carports

A freestanding carport stands on its own posts and footings. It still has to meet the boundary rules: at Stirling, posts at least 1 m from the side boundary to skip planning approval; at Wanneroo, 1 m (or 1.5 m if longer than 14 m), or on the boundary if no longer than 10 m and no higher than 2.7 m with the roof 500 mm back. Building code fire rules apply too: 900 mm from the boundary, or 500 mm if the carport is open.

Which is easier to approve?

Neither skips the permit. The planning rules are the same, so the choice usually comes down to your site, the look you want and the cost of the engineering. Use our front setback calculator if it's going in front of the house.

Questions

Common questions

Does an attached carport need a permit in WA?

Yes. Every carport needs a building permit, and an attached one needs connection details to the house and engineering.

Does a freestanding carport need a permit in WA?

Yes. The 10 m² exemption is for freestanding patios, sheds and gazebos, and even a minimum single carport (5.4 m x 2.4 m) is about 13 m².

Are the planning rules different for attached and freestanding carports?

No. Both have to meet the same street setback, side boundary, 60% frontage and open-view rules.

How close to the boundary can a freestanding carport be?

At Stirling, posts at least 1 m from the side boundary. Wanneroo allows posts on the boundary if the carport is no longer than 10 m and no higher than 2.7 m, with the roof 500 mm back.
