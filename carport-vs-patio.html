Carport vs patio: approvals compared

Short answer

Both usually need a building permit and follow the same side boundary rules. The big difference is the front yard: a carport can usually come forward into the front setback if it's no wider than 60% of the frontage and open to the street. A patio doesn't get that rule.

Last updated 19 November 2026

What's the difference?

The R-Codes define a carport as an open structure for parking one or more vehicles, with no door. A patio is an open structure with a solid (waterproof) roof, attached to the house or not. They can look the same, so councils go by what it's for and where it sits.

These come from the R-Codes (Volume 1, April 2026, clauses 5.1.3 and 5.2.1). Local policies can change them, so councils vary.

Which should you apply for?

Apply for what you'll actually use it for. If you'll park under it, call it a carport, because the carport rules let it sit closer to the street. If it's for outdoor living behind the house, call it a patio. Mislabelling it can cause trouble later, for example when you sell.

Questions

Common questions

What's the difference between a carport and a patio in WA?

A carport is an open structure for parking vehicles, with no door. A patio is an open structure with a solid roof, used for outdoor living.

Do carports and patios both need a building permit?

Usually yes. Only freestanding ones up to 10 m² and 2.4 m high are exempt.

Can a patio go in the front setback like a carport?

Not under the same rule. Carports have a special front setback rule. A patio in the front setback usually needs planning approval.

Can I use my patio as a carport?

If you'll park under it, it should be approved as a carport, which has its own front setback and width rules.
