How to fit a double carport on a narrow lot

Short answer

Measure your street frontage. Under the R-Codes, a carport can sit closer to the street only if it's no wider than 60% of the frontage, so a 6 m double carport needs about 10 m of frontage. If it's wider, set it back further, go tandem or use a rear lane. You'll usually also need a building permit.

Last updated 18 November 2026

Step 1: Measure your frontage

The key number is the width of your lot along the street (the frontage). Under the R-Codes (Volume 1, April 2026, clause 5.2.1 C1.2), a carport can sit closer to the street, by up to half the normal front setback, only if it's no wider than 60% of the frontage.

FrontageWidest carport for the reduced setback
8 m4.8 m
10 m6.0 m
12 m7.2 m
15 m9.0 m

So a 6 m wide double carport needs a frontage of at least 10 m to use the reduced setback. The carport must also leave a clear view between the house and the street, and match the house in roof pitch, colours and materials.

Step 2: Check the driveway

  • A driveway is usually 3 m to 6 m wide at the street boundary.
  • It should be at least 0.5 m from the side boundary and from any street pole.
  • Walls, fences and posts within 1.5 m of where the driveway meets the street must be no higher than 0.75 m, so drivers can see.

These come from R-Codes clauses 5.3.5 and 5.2.5.

Step 3: Pick the option that fits

OptionWhen it works
Double carport in the reduced setbackWidth is 60% of frontage or less
Double carport further backWider carport, set back to the full front setback
Tandem carport (one car behind the other)Narrow lots where a double won't fit
Carport off a rear laneLots with a right of way at the back

Some areas are stricter. For example, in the City of Bayswater's Character Protection Areas, lots with a frontage of 12 m or less can only have a single carport. If your design doesn't meet the standard rules, you can still apply for planning approval. Councils vary. Almost every carport also needs a building permit.

Work out your setback with the front setback calculator, read about a carport in the front setback, or see what goes on a carport site plan.

Sources

  • Residential Design Codes Volume 1 (April 2026), clauses 5.2.1, 5.2.5 and 5.3.5
  • Character Protection Areas Policy, City of Bayswater

Questions

Common questions

Can I fit a double carport on a narrow lot in Perth?

Often yes. To sit closer to the street, the carport can be no wider than 60% of the lot frontage. A 6 m double carport needs about a 10 m frontage.

How wide can my carport be on a 10 m frontage?

Up to 6 m if you want to use the reduced front setback. A wider carport has to meet the full front setback.

How wide can my driveway be?

Under the R-Codes, usually between 3 m and 6 m at the street boundary, and at least 0.5 m from the side boundary.

What if a double carport won't fit?

Consider a tandem carport, setting the carport further back, using a rear lane, or applying for planning approval.
